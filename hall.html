THE ROOM · SCREEN 1

Nine metres of cloth.
A room that goes quiet.

Everything the hall is, on one page: the lamp, the cloth, the two decks, the aisle and the booth. The frames below are not photographs of the building — they are what our projector throws at it.

01 · THE STAGE9.1 m × 4.7 m · matte white · no curve
    02 · THE BOOTHOne projector · one operator · one take

    The room is built
    around one chair.

    The booth is a two-metre room at the back of the balcony with a lamp in it and a person beside the lamp. There is no second projector to fail over, so the check before the first show is the whole safety net: focus on the eye close-up, sound at the middle of the stall, then the level again after the interval.

    01The lamp

    Calibrated on Mondays, against one frame.

    A 4K laser, chosen because it does not need a lamp change in the middle of a run. If the eye close-up is soft, the show does not start until it is not.

    02THE AISLE

    Two exits, both swept before the first show.

    Nobody walks the aisle once the feature starts — that is the rule the operator enforces, not the usher. The interval is twelve minutes, which is exactly enough for two people and a smoke.

    03THE SEATS

    380 stall, 240 balcony, all of them the same age.

    The back row of the stall is where the projectionist sits when the show is running well. That seat is not for sale, and it is the best one in the house.

    ARUSH CINEPLEX · MADHUBAN, BIHAR

    ONE SCREENAC

    Arush Cineplex,
    in brief.

    One auditorium on Station Road in Madhuban, Bihar. 620 seats across two decks, one 4K laser projector, one film at a time — three shows a day, a twelve minute interval, and a counter that takes cash from 9:00 AM. It is not a chain and it does not pretend to be: the person who checks the sound before the first show is often the person who sells your ticket.

The programme · THIS WEEK

Three shows a day.
Cash at the counter.

One film, twenty-one shows, and no choices to make beyond the deck you sit on. The board below is the week as it stands; the counter can and does overrule it.

THE FILM, IN PICTURES HOUSE RULES AND DIRECTIONS
01 · THE WEEKTwenty-one shows · four house fulls

The week, on one board.

Every show is the same film. The evening show on Friday, Saturday and Sunday is the one that fills — if the board says HOUSE FULL, the counter will still sell the next slot, and it will tell you honestly which one.

TICKETS AT The counter House full — waitlist at the counterDoors open 20 minutes before each show
02 · The counterOpens 9:00 AM · closes 10 minutes after the last show

Two prices, one queue,
no app.

    Half the tickets are sold before the first show — the morning house is the cheap one and the quarter-day crowd knows it. If you want the middle of the stall on the evening show, come an hour early, not ten minutes.

    —THE SMALL PRINT
    • Tickets are sold for the show, not the seat. No reservation, no holding.
    • No refunds once the feature has started. Exchange only at the manager’s desk, same day.
    • Group booking of ten or more: come to the counter the day before, not on the day.
    • Aadhaar is not required. A student card gets the half rate.
    01HOW IT FILLS

    The 7:00 PM is the show everybody wants.

    Monday to Wednesday you can walk up at 6:50. Friday to Sunday the queue starts at the bus stand and the balcony is the honest answer by 6:30.

    02THE INTERVAL

    Twelve minutes, one break, mid-show.

    The house lights come up for exactly that long. The counter stays open through it, and so does the aisle — the only time anyone is allowed to walk in front of the cloth.

    03SCHOOL AND GROUPS

    Ten or more, the day before.

    A teacher with a list gets the group rate and the morning house, which is cheaper for you and easier for us. There is no booking form; the counter writes it down.

    ONE SCREENAC

    Arush Cineplex,
    in brief.

    One auditorium on Station Road in Madhuban, Bihar. 620 seats across two decks, one 4K laser projector, one film at a time — three shows a day, a twelve minute interval, and a counter that takes cash from 9:00 AM. It is not a chain and it does not pretend to be: the person who checks the sound before the first show is often the person who sells your ticket.
